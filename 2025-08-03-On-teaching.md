<p>I just wrapped up teaching at a project based learning summer camp for six weeks, and Michelle and I are about to head on our first big international trip as a a couple. I figure now is a good time to reflect on both of those things.  </p>
<p><strong>On Teaching</strong>  </p>
<p>Teaching for me, honestly, is largely a series of very quick decisions, largely about ethics and safety. That&#39;s not what you put on the resume, of course, but that&#39;s what a lot of the actual bulk of the work is. A child comes up to you and asks if they can do something, and you have to decide if that thing is safe, and if it is ethical. If the answer to both of those questions is yes, then I&#39;m usually going to say yes. The worst that can happen is that they fail, which means they&#39;ll learn something. I wish teaching was about just depositing what I know into someone else&#39;s brain (the &quot;banking model&quot;) but that&#39;s not a very effective mode of learning. Most people remember what they did more than they remember what someone said to them. So the actual crux of teaching is designing problems to solve or questions to answer, not answering other people&#39;s questions. Then, when they run into problems with the problems or questions about the questions, you help as best you can.  </p>
<p>To that end, I noticed that students, especially in the two-week period I got to teach comics, loved drawing warm-ups that I thought might feel esoteric. Things like drawing a bizarre shape and turning it into a monster. Or drawing the same thing in 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds, 15 seconds. These are exercises I read in Lynda Barry syllabi or did in college classes. But it turns out that those mental challenges were totally accessible for 6-12 year old kids. I&#39;m continually surprised by what things are and aren&#39;t accessible to young people, <em>especially</em> kindergartners through 3rd graders. Their brains aren&#39;t just our brains but dumber! They&#39;re actually developmentally lopsided- parts of thinking arrive at different times. A second grader might not be able to comprehend the difference between 100 years in the past and 1000 years in the past! But they can comprehend things like war and grief. That said, once you get to fourth grade it seems they are just less experienced, soon to be more hormonal, people. Which makes teaching them much easier.  </p>
<p>Other things I noticed:</p>
<ul>
<li>Being loud is valuable</li>
<li>Being a man is valuable (sometimes). Largely because there&#39;s more women in teaching than men. Children can be tribal about their genders, and so being the only guy counselor at the camp ended up being invaluable, since it could be much easier for me to get through to a third grade boy.</li>
<li>There may just be bad people in this world. I had some kids who were given every choice to become more ethical, better community members, and they didn&#39;t.</li>
<li>There is something very rewarding, but very scary about seeing a kid go through something you went through. There was a camper, a young boy, who started to be socially ostracized basically because he was being annoying and inconsiderate without realizing it. He wasn&#39;t ill-intentioned. Nobody thought he was. But they started to really dislike him. And he started to feel it. And cry and cry and cry. Situations like that led me to be suicidal when I was his age. I did what I could to intervene with him and his fellow campers. I hope I did the right thing.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Traveling Abroad</strong>  </p>
<p>In four hours my girlfriend, Michelle, and her father will pick me up and we will drive to LAX. Michelle and I will get on a plane, when we get off the plane we will be in South-East Asia, where we will be until our plane lands in LAX on August 18th. We will go on a one-day adventure in Singapore. We will go to an Indian wedding. We will explore Hong Kong.  </p>
<p>The last time I left North America, it was 2015, and my mom&#39;s stomach cancer was getting bad. She would die the following November. In her final year, we went on a lovely set of vacations, including one to Europe, where we went to Paris, Edinburgh, and Dublin. We made a lot of wonderful memories on that trip.  </p>
<p>This is a big test of my relationship with Michelle. One that I&#39;m sure we&#39;ll pass with flying colors- we travel well together and are good communicators. I just have to focus on eating three meals a day and getting eight hours of sleep, which are the main things I need to feel normal. Every mistake I&#39;ve regretted for years was made during a period of time where I didn&#39;t get enough sleep. This trip gets to double as our two-year anniversary trip. Which is wonderful, this is the best relationship I&#39;ve ever been in by a country mile. I just got to make sure I take care of myself on this trip, so I also take care of the relationship.  </p>
<p>All the places we&#39;re going to are bad for free speech in some way. Singapore and Hong Kong most notably, but the Modi administration in India is nothing to sniff at. I don&#39;t think that will negatively affect our trip in a large way, but it is something I&#39;m keeping an eye on. I&#39;ve never been somewhere where saying the wrong thing on accident can get you thrown in prison. So I don&#39;t know how that will affect things.  </p>
<p>Here are some things that I&#39;m going to pay attention to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Large visual culture differences in each country. How big are the streets. What do trash cans look like. How are bathrooms different?</li>
<li>What counts as polite?</li>
<li>What comics do they read? Do they read comics?</li>
<li>How do they treat me vs how do they treat Michelle? Why?</li>
<li>How is the legacy of British colonialism different in each place? Singapore, Kolkata, and Hong Kong all have notable British influences to them.</li>
<li>To the extent that English is spoken, how is it different? How similar is it to American English?</li>
<li>How does the August of SEA (hot and humid) change how people act compared to the August of California (hot and dry)? How does it affect how we act?</li>
</ul>
<p>I plan on taking a lot of notes, drawings, and pictures. I would like to make headwind on the next comic, but I don&#39;t know how much pressure to put on myself in that regard. We&#39;ll see what happens. Time will happen to me and then, all of a sudden, the trip will be over. When I&#39;m back in California, I&#39;ll tell you how it went.</p>
